The Daodejing

What this activity covers

A gate station at a mountain pass west of the Yellow River valley. The keeper of the pass owns a copy of a short book, about five thousand characters, and tells the story of how the book came to be written at a station like this one. The activity covers the way that cannot be spoken, the usefulness of what is empty, acting without forcing, and the book’s quarrel with everything you heard in the courtyard at Lu. Press Continue to move through the scene, and answer each question when it appears. At the end you can review your responses, answer discussion questions, take a short quiz, and download a report of your work. You can skip any question you would rather not answer. In course mode, which your instructor may set for you, the last question asks for a written answer.

Discussion questions

1. The hub, the vessel, and the room repeat the argument of the dry garden about empty space. State what the two traditions are each doing with absence, and whether it is the same discovery made twice.

2. Water benefits all things without contending, and the soft outwears the hard. Name one real situation where the water-like approach plainly wins, and one where it plainly fails, and say what separates the two situations.

3. Govern a great state as you would cook a small fish, the book advises, that is, with minimal handling. Translate that into one concrete rule for any institution you know, and give the strongest case against your rule.
