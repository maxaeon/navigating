Confucius and the Rites

What this activity covers

A courtyard school in the state of Lu, around 484 BCE. An old teacher who never found a ruler to employ him receives students at home, and travelers may stand at the side. The activity covers humaneness and the rites, the mean in action, the rectification of names, and the rule of reciprocity, and it compares this teaching with the one you heard at the Lyceum, which arose independently about a century and a half later. Press Continue to move through the scene, and answer each question when it appears. Discussion questions

1. The Analects teaches in short sayings and remembered cases, and the Nicomachean Ethics teaches in a treatise of definitions and arguments. What does each form do well, and what does each form risk?

2. Confucius measures a person by their relationships, as a child, a parent, a minister, a friend. Aristotle measures by the rational activity of the individual soul. Describe one situation where the two standards would give different verdicts on the same person.

3. Confucius holds that goodness which must fight itself every hour is unfinished, and Aristotle likewise ranks the person who enjoys acting well above the person who acts well through gritted teeth. Is that demand realistic, and what does it imply about how much moral effort should show?
