Ban Zhao

What this activity covers

The imperial library at Luoyang, around 100 CE, under the Han dynasty. The scholar working here finished the official history of the dynasty after her father and brother died with it incomplete, taught the empress, and wrote a short book of instructions for women that has been argued about ever since. The activity covers finishing another’s work, her argument that daughters must be educated, and the problem of reading a thinker inside constraints. Discussion questions

1. Ban Zhao's tables and treatise circulate inside a book bearing her brother's name, and this journey has met the pattern before, in Alexandria and Baghdad. State a fair rule for crediting finishers, editors, and translators, and apply it to one modern case.

2. Later centuries made the Lessons an orthodoxy and enforced its instructions on women who had no say in the matter. Say how much responsibility an author bears for what later readers build from their book, and what, if anything, an author can do about it in advance.

3. Elisabeth of Bohemia, Hypatia, and Ban Zhao all worked at the edge of institutions that excluded them. Compare how each turned the position into philosophy, and what was different about what each could say aloud.
