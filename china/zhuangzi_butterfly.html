Zhuangzi's Butterfly

What this activity covers

A riverbank downstream of the valley, in the fourth century BCE. The man fishing here has declined a ministry, argues mostly by stories, and once dreamed he was a butterfly. The activity covers the dream, its meeting with the stove-heated room you visited in Europe, the useless tree, and the debate about the happiness of fish. Press Continue to move through the scene, and answer each question when it appears. At the end you can review your responses, answer discussion questions, take a short quiz, and download a report of your work. You can skip any question you would rather not answer. In course mode, which your instructor may set for you, the last question asks for a written answer.

Discussion questions

1. In the fish debate, Huizi objects that Zhuangzi cannot know the fish are happy, and Zhuangzi turns the objection on the objector. Reconstruct both moves as arguments about knowing other minds, and say who wins the exchange.

2. The useless tree is left alone by the axe. Name one person, practice, or discipline that survives by being useless to power, and one destroyed by being useful to it, and draw the lesson carefully.

3. Zhuangzi argues almost entirely in stories, where the courtyard argued in maxims and the Greeks in premises. Say what a story can establish that a premise cannot, and what it cannot establish that a premise can.
