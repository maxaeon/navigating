The Prison of Socrates

What this activity covers

Athens, 399 BC, a month after the verdict. The execution has waited because the city's sacred ship was at sea on its mission to Delos, and no execution is allowed until it returns. Yesterday it came in. You cast a ballot at the trial, and today you go to the state prison, where the jailer admits the visitors. Inside, his friends want him to escape, and he answers them. Walk with the arrow keys, press E to talk and to take a place among the visitors, and answer each question when it appears. At the end you can review your responses, answer discussion questions, take a short quiz, and download a report of your work. You can skip any question you would rather not answer. In course mode, which your instructor may set for you, the last question asks for a written answer.

Discussion questions

1. Compare Socrates' position, obey the law or persuade the city to change it, with a modern movement that broke laws openly and accepted arrest. Are these the same position or different ones? Say where they agree and where they part.

2. The Laws argue that by staying in Athens for seventy years, Socrates agreed to obey them. Does living somewhere amount to agreeing to its laws? What would real agreement require?

3. Our detailed account of this day comes from Plato, who records that he was not there. How should a careful reader weigh a source that is honest about its own distance from the events?
