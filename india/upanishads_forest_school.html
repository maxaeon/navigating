The Self in the Upanishads

What this activity covers

A forest school on the Ganges plain, in the centuries before the Buddha. A father is teaching his son the doctrine that stands at the end of the Vedas, and the lesson uses a fig, a bowl of salt water, and a question about what the word I points to. You take part in the demonstrations. Press Continue to move through the scene, and answer each question when it appears. At the end you can review your responses, answer discussion questions, take a short quiz, and download a report of your work. You can skip any question you would rather not answer. In course mode, which your instructor may set for you, the last question asks for a written answer.

Discussion questions

1. Uddalaka teaches by demonstration, a fig and a bowl of salt water, rather than by definition. Compare his method with the sand lesson at the grove of Akademos. What does each method assume about where knowledge comes from?

2. In the beginning this was Being alone, one only, without a second. The sentence stands near Parmenides. Compare what each teacher does next, since Parmenides denies motion and change while Uddalaka keeps the changing world as names and shapes of the one. Which of the two handles ordinary experience better?

3. If tat tvam asi were true, what, if anything, should change in how a person treats other people? Trace the step from the metaphysics to the ethics, and mark where the step is weakest.
