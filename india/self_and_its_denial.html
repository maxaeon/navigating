The Self and Its Denial

What this activity covers

A deer park at Sarnath, near Varanasi, in roughly the fifth century BCE. The teacher here examines the claim you heard at the forest school and denies it, and the denial is meant to be checked rather than believed. The activity includes a short attention exercise, used as a method of philosophy, examining your own experience to test a claim, the way Descartes examined the piece of wax. It is not religious instruction, no practice of any tradition is taught, and every step of it can be passed through with Continue. Press Continue to move through the scene, and answer each question when it appears. At the end you can review your responses, answer discussion questions, take a short quiz, and download a report of your work. You can skip any question you would rather not answer. In course mode, which your instructor may set for you, the last question asks for a written answer.

Discussion questions

1. The carpenter on the Theseus and Nagasena's chariot take the same problem apart with the same move, parts and a name for their assembly. Say whether the ship's question and the chariot's question can receive different answers, and why.

2. The exercise asked you to find the observer. State what would count as finding one, and then say whether the test is fair, since some philosophers argue that the observer could never appear as an object to itself, the way an eye does not appear in its own field of view.

3. The Upanishads and the Buddha disagree about the self and agree that holding tightly to what changes produces suffering. Explain how the same practical claim can stand on opposite metaphysics.
