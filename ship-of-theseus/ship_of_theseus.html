The Ship of Theseus

What this activity covers

Your travels in Ionia are over, and your jury year in Athens is about to begin. To pay for the passage home, you have taken a job as ship's carpenter aboard the Theseus, an old wooden merchant ship crossing the Aegean from Miletus to Athens. Wooden ships rot and break, and your work is to replace what fails, port by port. As the original timbers pile up on the docks behind you, everyone aboard has the same question: is this still the same ship? Walk the deck with the arrow keys, press E to talk and to repair, and answer each question when it appears. At the end you can review your responses, answer discussion questions, take a short quiz, and download a report of your work. You can skip any question you would rather not answer. In course mode, which your instructor may set for you, the last question asks for a written answer.

Discussion questions

1. Apply the puzzle to something that is not a ship: a band that has replaced every member, a rebuilt team, a website whose code has all been rewritten. Is it the same one? Say why.

2. Most of the cells in your body have been replaced since childhood. Are you the person who started your first year of school? What makes the answer yes or no?

3. Suppose a court must decide which vessel owes the harbor fees, and a museum must decide which one to display. Could they reasonably give different answers? What would that show about the question?
