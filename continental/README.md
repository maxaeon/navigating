# The Continental Room

Philosophy that stays close to lived experience, asking what it is like to be a person in a situation before asking what a person is made of. This room and The Analytic Room are two habits of doing philosophy rather than two sets of doctrines, and each region map links sideways to the others so a student can move between periods without returning to the world map.

## The Myth of Sisyphus

Algiers, 1942, on the road above the harbour. Camus defines the absurd carefully, as the meeting between a mind that wants sense and a world that returns none, and insists it belongs to neither side alone. At the tram stop the daily rhythm produces the question. At the cemetery wall he closes off two ways out, and names the second philosophical suicide, keeping the questioner while quietly putting down the question. At the top of the hill he gives what follows, revolt, freedom, and passion, without claiming the essay proves anyone can live that way. At the boulder two close views show the climb and then the descent, and the student is asked whether the rock becomes his.

The activity discusses suicide only as the philosophical question Camus opens with, briefly and without any detail of method. The description behind the title says so before a student begins, tells them they may skip that stop or the activity, and suggests speaking to someone they trust. No prompt asks a student anything personal about the subject.
