The Five Ways

What this activity covers

The unit's road jumps two centuries, as this region does on purpose: it is now around 1270, and in the abbey church a visiting Dominican master named Thomas Aquinas is walking the aisles before vespers. Where the prior of Bec argued from a definition alone, Aquinas argues from things you can point at, and the church is full of them. You will walk the church with him, station to station: the bell rope, the candles, a memorial slab, the light in the windows, and the swallows under the eaves, and each one becomes one of his five arguments. Walk with the arrow keys, press E at each marked station, and answer each question when it appears. At the end you can review your responses, answer discussion questions, take a short quiz, and download a report of your work. You can skip any question you would rather not answer. In course mode, which your instructor may set for you, the last question asks for a written answer.

Discussion questions

1. A standing objection, pressed later by Hume and Russell: if every member of a series has a cause, why does the whole series need one more? Explain the objection in your own words, and say what Aquinas would need to show to answer it.

2. Modern cosmology gives the universe a history and possibly a beginning. Does that help the first cause argument, hurt it, or change the subject? Say why.

3. Each Way ends with a phrase like "and this all understand to be God." Describe the gap between a first mover or first cause and the God people actually worship, and what further argument would be needed to cross it.
