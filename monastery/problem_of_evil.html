The Problem of Evil

What this activity covers

The abbey's infirmary, on the same winter night. The infirmarer, the monk who nurses the sick, has heard the arguments of the scriptorium and the church, and he has a question of his own, and he asks it where he works: among the beds. If God is all good and all powerful, the infirmary needs explaining. You will walk the hall with him, hear the problem in its oldest form, and test the three most famous answers against what is in front of you. Walk with the arrow keys, press E at each marked bedside, and answer each question when it appears. At the end you can review your responses, answer discussion questions, take a short quiz, and download a report of your work. The scenes here concern illness and loss, handled with restraint. You can skip any question you would rather not answer. In course mode, which your instructor may set for you, the last question asks for a written answer.

Discussion questions

1. Theodicies explain suffering in general, and bedsides hold particular sufferers. Describe the difference between answering the problem of evil in a seminar and answering it beside a sick friend, and what each setting owes the other.

2. Mackie asked why an all-powerful God could not have made free creatures who always freely choose the good. Explain his point in your own words, and give the best reply you can construct on the other side.

3. Rowe's evidential argument rests on suffering that appears to serve no purpose at all. Can anyone be in a position to judge that suffering is pointless, and can anyone be in a position to judge that it is not? What follows from your answers?
