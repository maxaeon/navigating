Anselm's Argument

What this activity covers

Normandy, 1078, deep winter. The journey has left Greece far behind, and you arrive at the abbey of Bec at dusk, seeking shelter. The prior, a monk named Anselm, has just finished a small book called the Proslogion, and in it is an argument that has been making believers and doubters argue ever since: a proof of God from a definition alone, with no observation of the world required. He will walk you through it step by step, you will grant or resist each step, and a letter from a monk named Gaunilo waits in the day's mail. Walk with the arrow keys, press E to talk, and answer each question when it appears. At the end you can review your responses, answer discussion questions, take a short quiz, and download a report of your work. You can skip any question you would rather not answer. In course mode, which your instructor may set for you, the last question asks for a written answer.

Discussion questions

1. Centuries later, Kant objected that existence is not a property a thing can have or lack the way it has a color or a size, so adding existence cannot make a concept greater. Explain the objection in your own words, and say whether you find it convincing.

2. Has an argument ever changed what you believe about something important, as opposed to how you talk about it? What did the change require besides the argument?

3. Anselm and Gaunilo were both believing monks, and their exchange is sharp but respectful. What does their debate suggest about the difference between defending a belief and testing one?
