The Ring of Gyges

What this activity covers

A night camp on the road east, in the hill country the Greeks called Lydia. This is a situation on the road. A storyteller at the fire retells the oldest thought experiment about justice, from the second book of Plato’s Republic, and the tale ends by asking what you would do. Press Continue to move through the scene, and answer each question when it appears. At the end you can review your responses, answer discussion questions, take a short quiz, and download a report of your work. You can skip any question you would rather not answer. In course mode, which your instructor may set for you, the last question asks for a written answer.

Discussion questions

1. Ordinary life hands out partial invisibility, in anonymous accounts, in crowds, and at a distance from the people affected. Name one such place, describe how people behave there, and say whether the behavior supports Glaucon's claim.

2. Audits, cameras, and checks on power are institutions designed for people holding small rings. Would you design institutions for the people Glaucon describes or for the people who would stay just, and what does each design cost?

3. Socrates answers the tale by arguing that injustice damages the person who does it, whatever the audience, as illness damages a body. State that idea in your own words and give one piece of evidence for or against it from lives you have seen.
