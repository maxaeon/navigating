The Bridge Crossing

What this activity covers

A mountain pass on the road between regions, at dusk. This is a situation on the road. It was not on your itinerary, and it will not wait for a convenient moment. Below the footbridge runs a rail line, and something is wrong on it. You faced a version of this once before, at a lever, in the prologue. This version is harder, and the difference between the two versions is what this activity examines. Press Continue to move through the scene, and answer each question when it appears. At the end you can review your responses, answer discussion questions, take a short quiz, and download a report of your work. You can skip any question you would rather not answer. In course mode, which your instructor may set for you, the last question asks for a written answer.

Discussion questions

1. Design a third variant of the situation that isolates exactly one difference from the footbridge, physical contact, intention, or the use of a person as a means, and predict how people would answer it.

2. Self-driving vehicles, triage protocols, and military targeting rules all encode answers to cases like these in advance. Should such answers be written down before the emergency, and by whom?

3. Suppose someone answers the lever and the footbridge differently and cannot state a principle that separates them. Is that a defect in their reasoning, or evidence that morality does not reduce to statable principles?
