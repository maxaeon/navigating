Avicenna's Flying Man

What this activity covers

Hamadan, in Persia, around 1020, late at night. The vizier of the city practices medicine and politics by day and writes philosophy after dark, and travelers with questions are received after dark too. The activity covers the most famous thought experiment of the falsafa tradition, a person created in mid-air who senses nothing, and the question of what such a person would still know. Press Continue to move through the scene, and answer each question when it appears. At the end you can review your responses, answer discussion questions, take a short quiz, and download a report of your work. You can skip any question you would rather not answer. In course mode, which your instructor may set for you, the last question asks for a written answer.

Discussion questions

1. Design a change to the flying man that would answer the standard objection, or explain why no change can answer it. Say exactly what the changed case would show.

2. Descartes reached a similar landing point six centuries later, one certainty surviving total doubt. Compare the two routes, a thought experiment about creation against a method of doubt, and say which route earns its conclusion at a lower cost.

3. The deer park and Hamadan both appeal to what awareness finds. Propose a way to make such appeals answerable to each other, a shared test, a shared vocabulary, or something else, or argue that none is possible.
