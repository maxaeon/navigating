Al-Ghazali's Challenge

What this activity covers

Baghdad, 1095, at the Nizamiyya college. The most celebrated professor in the city is finishing a book against the philosophers and preparing to leave his post, and the argument at the center of the book concerns a piece of cotton held to a flame. The activity connects that argument to one you walked in Edinburgh, made independently six and a half centuries later. Press Continue to move through the scene, and answer each question when it appears. At the end you can review your responses, answer discussion questions, take a short quiz, and download a report of your work. You can skip any question you would rather not answer. In course mode, which your instructor may set for you, the last question asks for a written answer.

Discussion questions

1. Al-Ghazali and Hume draw different conclusions from one observation. State what evidence, if any, could decide between habit in us and custom of God, or explain why the choice lies beyond evidence.

2. Al-Ghazali warned believers against attacking demonstrated mathematics and astronomy, arguing that bad attacks discredit the attacker's religion rather than the science. State the principle behind that warning and apply it to one modern dispute.

3. A blanket claim that al-Ghazali ended science in the Islamic world circulates widely, and historians point to observatories and mathematicians flourishing after him. Say what evidence would be needed to establish or retire a claim of that size, and why such claims outrun their evidence so easily.
