The Translation Movement

What this activity covers

Baghdad in the 830s, at the height of the Abbasid caliphate. The traveler arrives carrying books, which turns out to be the right thing to carry. The activity visits the translators who moved Greek philosophy and science into Arabic, the mathematician whose book named algebra, and the philosopher who defended taking truth from any source. Press Continue to move through the scene, and answer each question when it appears. At the end you can review your responses, answer discussion questions, take a short quiz, and download a report of your work. You can skip any question you would rather not answer. In course mode, which your instructor may set for you, the last question asks for a written answer.

Discussion questions

1. Name the closest thing your own time has to the translation movement, and apply Hunayn's two rules to it, comparing the copies and carrying the sense. What would following those rules change?

2. When Aristotle reached Europe in Latin, the Arabic commentaries arrived with him, and Europe's first arguments about him were arguments with those commentators. Can a text ever travel without interpretation attached, and would a bare text even be usable?

3. Hypatia's editions, Hunayn's collations, and the copyists between them are mostly unnamed in what survives. Whose names tend to survive transmission and whose are lost, and does the pattern matter?
