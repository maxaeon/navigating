The Trial of Socrates

What this activity covers

Athens, 399 BC. Your jury year has brought its first allotment, and it is the case the whole city is talking about: Socrates has been indicted, and you are one of the 501 citizens that will hear the case today. On the way to the court you will pass through the Agora, where he has spent his life asking questions, and you will see the method itself at work before you judge the man. In the court you will hear the charges and the defense, and you will cast a real vote. Walk with the arrow keys, press E to talk, and answer each question when it appears. At the end you can review your responses, answer discussion questions, take a short quiz, and download a report of your work. You can skip any question you would rather not answer. In course mode, which your instructor may set for you, the last question asks for a written answer.

Discussion questions

1. Should there be any limits on what teachers may teach or ask in public? If so, who should decide where the limits sit, and how could that power be abused?

2. Describe a modern case where a person's public image, built up over years, decided their fate before any formal judgment did. What could that person have done about it?

3. Socrates refused to bring his weeping family before the jury or to beg, saying a juror should be persuaded, not moved to pity. Was that integrity, arrogance, or both? Say why.
