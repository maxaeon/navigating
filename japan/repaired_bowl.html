The Repaired Bowl

What this activity covers

A lacquer workshop in the craft quarter of Kyoto, around 1600. A tea bowl has arrived in pieces, with a letter from its owner, and the craftsman repairs such things in a way that hides nothing. The activity covers kintsugi, the golden repair, and brings it to the questions about identity and the self carried from the Aegean, the deer park, and the ford. Press Continue to move through the scene, and answer each question when it appears. At the end you can review your responses, answer discussion questions, take a short quiz, and download a report of your work. You can skip any question you would rather not answer. In course mode, which your instructor may set for you, the last question asks for a written answer.

Discussion questions

1. Museums restore paintings so the restoration disappears, and some buildings keep their patches visible. Propose a criterion for when a repair should show and when it should not, and test it on two cases of your own.

2. In the tea culture of the period, some mended bowls were named, recorded, and valued above unbroken ones. Is that a discovery about value or a fashion of one culture and time? Say what evidence could tell the two apart.

3. Apply the deer park's claim to the workshop. If objects and persons are processes rather than fixed things, state what the words damage and repair mean, and whether anything is lost by giving up the fixed thing.
