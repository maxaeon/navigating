The Practice of the Koan

What this activity covers

A Rinzai Zen training hall in Japan, in the tradition given its lasting shape by Hakuin in the eighteenth century. Travelers may observe the hall, and a senior monk explains the institution to visitors. The activity studies the koan as a method, the way the journey studied the theater and the garden. It teaches no practice, and it compares the method with the questioning you watched in the Athenian agora. Press Continue to move through the scene, and answer each question when it appears. At the end you can review your responses, answer discussion questions, take a short quiz, and download a report of your work. You can skip any question you would rather not answer. In course mode, which your instructor may set for you, the last question asks for a written answer.

Discussion questions

1. The koan and the Socratic question both use questioning to change the answerer. Write one question of each kind about the same subject, and say what each of your questions is built to do.

2. The master's examination is the tradition's criterion for genuine insight. Name one other field that certifies an inner state or ability through an examiner, and compare how much trust each criterion earns and why.

3. The Daodejing opened by warning that words cannot carry the way, and the koan is a use of words built against their ordinary use. Say whether language can be used to point past language, and what your answer means for philosophy, which has nothing else to work with.
