The Dry Garden

What this activity covers

A temple veranda at the edge of Kyoto, around 1500. Before you is a garden with no plants in it, fifteen stones in a rectangle of raked gravel. The activity studies the garden as a work of art, the way the journey studied the Theater of Dionysus, and it teaches no practice. It includes a short placement exercise done by pressing on the scene. Press Continue to move through the scene, and answer each question when it appears. At the end you can review your responses, answer discussion questions, take a short quiz, and download a report of your work. You can skip any question you would rather not answer. In course mode, which your instructor may set for you, the last question asks for a written answer.

Discussion questions

1. Name one object that is beautiful by the classical standard of proportion and symmetry and plain by the standards of this garden, and one object where the reverse holds. Say what each judgment is tracking.

2. The temple keeps no official meaning for the garden, and visitors have supplied many. Is the meaning of an artwork fixed by its maker, by its tradition, or by its viewers? Use the garden and one western artwork you know in your answer.

3. The monk treats empty space as a material. Name an art that is not visual, writing, music, or conversation, where what is left out does real work, and describe how it does it.
