The Roman World

About this page

Rome absorbed the Greek world and inherited its philosophy, then put a different question to it. The Greeks asked what the world is made of and what the good life consists in. The schools of the empire were asked something more pressing by people with less control over their circumstances. How do I stay steady when the world does what it likes with me? Three answers competed, and all three promised peace of mind. The Stoics located it in the distinction between what is up to us and what is not. The Skeptics located it in setting arguments against arguments until judgment lifts. The Platonists, still teaching in Alexandria three centuries on, located it in the ascent toward what does not change. The stops below run in the order the ideas met each other, and the region jumps centuries between them.
