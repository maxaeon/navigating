Hypatia of Alexandria

What this activity covers

The expedition crosses the sea, and the calendar moves to around the year 410. Alexandria has been a Roman city for four centuries, and its most famous teacher is Hypatia, mathematician, astronomer, and head of the city's Platonist school, who lectures publicly to pagans and Christians alike. A student of hers named Synesius meets you at the harbor and walks you through the city to her hall, where the evening's lesson covers what philosophy became under Rome, the Stoic road, the Skeptic road, and her own Platonist road, three schools that all promise peace of mind and disagree about how to reach it. Walk with the arrow keys, press E at each marked stop, and answer each question when it appears. At the end you can review your responses, answer discussion questions, take a short quiz, and download a report of your work.

Discussion questions

1. Epictetus taught that some things are up to us and some are not, and that misery comes from confusing the two. Test the claim against a real difficulty in your own life. What does the division illuminate, and what does it leave out?

2. Sextus Empiricus claimed that for every argument an equally strong opposing argument can be found, and that suspending judgment brings tranquility. Is suspension of judgment actually livable? Identify a belief you could not suspend and still function, and say what that shows about skepticism.

3. Hypatia lectured publicly in a city dividing along religious lines, and her classroom held students from both sides. What conditions does philosophy need from a city in order to exist, and what, if anything, does it owe the city in return?
