The School of Epictetus

What this activity covers

Nicopolis, in Greece, around the year 108. The philosophers were expelled from Rome years ago, and the most sought-after teacher of the age now runs a plain school here that Romans sail across the Adriatic to attend. Epictetus was born enslaved, was freed, and walks with a permanent limp. He teaches Stoicism as a set of exercises rather than a system to admire, and he is famously hard on students who take notes and change nothing. You will sit through a morning session and be asked the questions he asks. Walk with the arrow keys, press E at each marked stop, and answer each question when it appears. At the end you can review your responses, answer discussion questions, take a short quiz, and download a report of your work.

Discussion questions

1. Critics argue that the division between what is and is not up to us understates how much our inner lives are shaped by circumstances we did not choose, including poverty, illness, and how we were raised. Assess that criticism. Can the Stoic answer it without giving up the core claim?

2. Stoicism is widely marketed today as a productivity and resilience method. Compare that use with what Epictetus actually taught, and identify what is preserved and what is lost.

3. Epictetus says we should want events to happen as they do happen. Is that wisdom, or does it risk accepting conditions that should be resisted? Give a case where you think acceptance is right and one where you think it is wrong, and say what distinguishes them.
