Berkeley and the Tree in the Quad

What this activity covers

A short stop between the two roads, and the strangest conclusion in the region. It is 1710 at Trinity College, Dublin, and George Berkeley is twenty five and about to publish a book that will convince people he is mad. He begins from a premise Locke and Hume both accept, that all we have to work with is what the senses deliver, and refuses to stop applying it when it becomes inconvenient. You will put your hands in a basin, look at a coin from two angles, and end up under a tree being asked whether it needs you. Walk with the arrow keys, press E at each marked spot, and answer each question when it appears. At the end you can review your responses, answer discussion questions, take a short quiz, and download a report of your work. You can skip any question you would rather not answer. In course mode, which your instructor may set for you, the last question asks for a written answer.

Discussion questions

1. Berkeley insisted he was defending common sense, not attacking it. Read what he actually claims in this activity, then argue either that the label fits or that it does not.

2. His account of why our perceptions agree with each other rests on God. Try to construct a version of his position that explains the same agreement without appealing to God, and say what it loses.

3. Locke, Berkeley, and Hume all begin from the same premise about the senses and arrive at three different places. What does that suggest about the premise, about the reasoning, or about both?
