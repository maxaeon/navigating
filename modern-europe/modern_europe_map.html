Early Modern European Philosophy

About this page

One question organizes this region. Where does knowledge come from? The rationalists answer that reason, working from ideas the mind brings with it, can establish truths about reality, and mathematics is their model. The empiricists answer that every material of knowledge arrives through the senses, and experiment is theirs. Descartes opens the rationalist road in a stove-heated room by doubting everything the senses ever told him. Hume drives the empiricist road to its unsettling end in Edinburgh. Kant, reading Hume in Königsberg, attempts to show what each side saw and what each missed. And Wollstonecraft turns the era's own instrument, reason, on the era itself. The stops run in that order, and like the Monastery, this region jumps decades between stops and says so plainly at each one.
