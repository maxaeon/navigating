Hume in Edinburgh

What this activity covers

The other road through this region, and it starts where Descartes refused to. It is 1748, and David Hume has just rewritten his unread first book into something shorter and harder to ignore. He takes the senses as the only source of material the mind has, follows that assumption honestly, and arrives somewhere uncomfortable. You will walk the city with him while he lays out three steps: an inventory of what is in the mind, a look at what you actually observe when one thing causes another, and a question about tomorrow that he cannot answer and does not pretend to. Walk with the arrow keys, press E at each marked spot, and answer each question when it appears. At the end you can review your responses, answer discussion questions, take a short quiz, and download a report of your work. You can skip any question you would rather not answer. In course mode, which your instructor may set for you, the last question asks for a written answer.

Discussion questions

1. Hume grants that we cannot stop expecting the future to resemble the past, and denies that any argument supports it. Is a belief we cannot give up, and cannot justify, in good standing? Give a reason either way.

2. Science depends on inference from observed cases to unobserved ones. Describe how a working scientist might respond to Hume, and whether the response answers him or changes the subject.

3. Hume applies one test throughout: for any idea, which impression did it come from? Pick a word used seriously in public argument, apply his test to it, and report what you find.
