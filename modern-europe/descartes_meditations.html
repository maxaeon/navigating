Descartes and the Stove-Heated Room

What this activity covers

A new region, and a new question. The Monastery asked what can be proved about God. This region asks where knowledge comes from at all. It is winter 1619, and a young French soldier named Rene Descartes is shut in a warm room with a stove, working out a plan to rebuild everything he believes from the ground up. You will walk that plan with him, one doubt at a time. He will show you a stick that bends in water, ask you to watch a piece of wax change into something unrecognizable, and then push the doubt as far as it will go. Walk with the arrow keys, press E at each marked spot, and answer each question when it appears. At the end you can review your responses, answer discussion questions, take a short quiz, and download a report of your work. You can skip any question you would rather not answer. In course mode, which your instructor may set for you, the last question asks for a written answer.

Discussion questions

1. Descartes rebuilds knowledge from one certainty he cannot doubt. Pick a belief you hold that matters to you, and try his method on it. What survives the doubt, and what does that tell you about the method?

2. The wax argument claims the mind, not the senses, grasps what a thing is. Take an ordinary object that changes over time, a tree, a river, a friendship, and ask what makes it the same thing throughout. Does your answer support Descartes or work against him?

3. The rationalists start from ideas the mind supplies, and the empiricists start from what the senses deliver. Describe one thing you know, and trace where the knowledge came from. Does your case fit one road better than the other?
