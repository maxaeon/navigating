Two Objections

What this activity covers

An optional follow-up, in two places and two centuries. It begins in May 1643 at the exiled Bohemian court at The Hague, in the study of Princess Elisabeth, who has read the Meditations closely and is drafting a letter about a problem she does not think Descartes has solved. You will help her build the objection, watch what he sends back, and judge whether his replies answer her. The road then continues to Halle in 1734, where Anton Wilhelm Amo, born on the Guinea coast and now teaching in a German university, is defending a disputation that attacks the same claim from the opposite side. Elisabeth and Amo never met and could not have, since she died more than twenty years before he was born, and the activity says so. Walk with the arrow keys, press E at each marked spot, and answer each question when it appears. At the end you can review your responses, answer discussion questions, take a short quiz, and download a report of your work. You can skip any question you would rather not answer. In course mode, which your instructor may set for you, the last question asks for a written answer.

Discussion questions

1. Elisabeth is often described as a correspondent of Descartes rather than as a philosopher in her own right. Read what she actually argues here and assess that description.

2. The interaction problem is still discussed today. Set out one modern position on the relationship between mind and body, and say how it answers or avoids Elisabeth's question.

3. Amo and Elisabeth both read a system they had no part in building, and both found the same weak joint from outside it. What does this suggest about who is well placed to test an argument, and why?
