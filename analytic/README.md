# The Analytic Room

Philosophy done by taking claims apart, testing them against clear cases, and keeping arguments short enough to check. This room and The Continental Room are two habits of doing philosophy rather than two sets of doctrines, and each region map links sideways to the others.

## Knowledge, and Three Awkward Cases

An empty seminar room with the definition chalked on the board: knowledge is justified true belief. Three counterexamples follow, each with its own close view. The wall clock stopped yesterday at ten past nine and it happens to be ten past nine now, so the student believed truly for an ordinarily good reason and almost nobody wants to say they knew. Through the near window a white shape in the field is a rock, while a real sheep stands out of sight behind a rise, so the reason concerns one thing and the truth concerns another. Through the far window a valley holds a row of barn fronts left by a film crew with one real barn among them, which shifts the trouble from inside the student's head to the surrounding countryside.

The lecturer then lists the standard repairs, that no falsehood may do the work, that belief must track the truth, that the process must be reliable, and notes that each has counterexamples in the literature. The final question asks the student to repair the definition and then break their own repair.

The cases are credited in the activity: the stopped clock to Russell, the sheep to Chisholm, the barns to Goldman by way of Ginet, and the pattern to Gettier's paper of 1963.

## Mary in the Black and White Room

A viewing gallery above a monochrome laboratory. Mary has every physical fact about colour vision and has never seen a colour. At the viewing window a close view lists what she holds, every wavelength, every retinal process, every neural pathway, and everything ever written about red, with no colour anywhere in the room. At the door a second close view shows the step out, Mary still grey in the doorway and a single red tomato on a plain table beyond it. The student answers whether she learns anything, and either answer is followed through: if she learns, the three line argument bites, and if she does not, they owe an account of the very strong intuition.

At the seminar table three colleagues give the standard replies and disagree with each other, the ability reply, the acquaintance reply, and the same fact grasped in a new way. The activity closes with the fact students find useful and philosophers find awkward, that Frank Jackson later rejected the argument he became famous for, and draws the lesson that it is possible to follow objections against yourself.
