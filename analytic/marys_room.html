Mary in the Black and White Room

What this activity covers

A viewing gallery above a room with no colour in it, and a three line argument that has occupied philosophy of mind for forty years. Mary has lived her whole life in monochrome and is the world's leading authority on colour vision, holding every physical fact about light, eyes, and brains. This morning she walks out and sees a ripe tomato. If she learns something, then there was a fact she did not have, and she had every physical fact. You will watch the step out, hear the three standard replies from people who disagree with each other, and be told what the argument's author later decided about it. Walk with the arrow keys, press E at each marked spot, and answer each question when it appears. At the end you can review your responses, answer discussion questions, take a short quiz, and download a report of your work. You can skip any question you would rather not answer. In course mode, which your instructor may set for you, the last question asks for a written answer.

Discussion questions

1. Design your own version of the case using a sense other than sight, and say whether it produces the same intuition as strongly. What does the comparison suggest?

2. The ability reply says Mary gains know-how rather than knowledge that something is so. Give an example of each from ordinary life, then say whether what Mary gains fits either cleanly.

3. Jackson rejected his own argument on the grounds that the intuition is produced by how we represent our own experience. Is a strong and widely shared intuition evidence, and if so, how much?
